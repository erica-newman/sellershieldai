01 — Google Ads Conversion Tracking
Needs Attention

Conversion Tracking

Your account currently tracks two conversion actions — both sourced from HubSpot via click imports. Tracking is active, but there are gaps worth addressing before optimizing further.

Google Ads · Conversion Actions · Active conversion setup

Issue: Volume Discrepancy

HubSpot – Marketing Qualified Lead shows 36 conversions vs. HubSpot – Lead at 33. MQLs should be a subset of Leads — if Leads are fewer than MQLs, the tracking definition or import logic needs review.

Missing: Phone Call Tracking

Your phone number is a primary CTA on the website. CallRail call tracking isn't imported into Google Ads. Even as a secondary action, this helps identify any high-intent users converting via phone rather than form.

Current Setup

  • HubSpot – MQL: 90-day click window · Primary · 36 conv · $0 value
  • HubSpot – Lead: 30-day click window · Primary · 33 conv · $33 value
  • Both included in account-level goals
  • Source: Website import from clicks

Recommended Additions

  • Audit the HubSpot MQL vs. Lead definition — ensure Leads ≥ MQLs at all times
  • Import CallRail phone call conversions as a secondary action
  • Consider assigning conversion values to MQLs to enable value-based bidding down the road
  • Review 90-day window on MQL — does this match your sales cycle?
02 — Google Ads Bid Strategy
Opportunity

Bid Strategy

Your brand campaign is running a Target CPA bid strategy. For brand terms, this creates unnecessary friction — you should own your brand name at all times.

The Issue

Brand campaign (Luna_GSN_Brand) is set to Maximize Conversions (Target CPA: $60) on a $64/day budget. When the Target CPA constrains spend near the daily cap, Google may not show your ad for every brand search — even against no competition.

Brand searches are already qualified. Anyone searching your name is already aware of you. Restricting spend here with a CPA floor is counterproductive.

Recommendation

  • Test Maximize Conversions (no Target CPA) for the brand campaign
  • Monitor impression share — target 90%+ on brand terms
  • Keep Target CPA strategy for non-brand if/when you relaunch it

03 — Google Ads Campaign Settings
Minor Fix

Campaign Settings

A small but worth-noting setting on your PMax campaign that limits Google's ability to optimize delivery timing.

Ad Schedule on PMax

Your PMax campaign is set to run only 5:00 AM – 11:00 PM. Performance Max campaigns use Google's AI to determine optimal delivery timing across all placements. Applying a manual schedule overrides this and may suppress conversions that would otherwise occur outside those hours.

This is a lower-priority fix but worth testing: remove the ad schedule and let Google optimize delivery timing across the full day.

Also Worth Noting

  • PMax brand guidelines are set (Prepory, 3 logos) — good
  • Asset optimization is on — good
  • No brand exclusion list active — covered in the PMax section

04 — Google Ads Search Expansion
Largest Opportunity

Search: Expanding Beyond Brand

Brand search captures demand that already exists. Non-brand search creates demand — and this is the most significant untapped opportunity in your Google account.

A non-brand search campaign was paused in October 2025. At the time, it showed a cost per MQL of approximately $218. With some cleanup on search terms and a more structured ad group approach, this is a strong foundation to build from.

Ad Group 1 — BOF (Restart)

High purchase intent. Restart the paused campaign with tighter negative keyword controls.

Ad Group 2 — Grad School

Less competition, strong purchase intent. Especially worth testing if grad school is a meaningful revenue segment.

MBA admissions consultant law school admissions consulting grad school admissions consultant med school consultant

Ad Group 3 — MOF / Awareness

Broader audience, lower intent but high volume. Use to build the funnel — especially valuable for retargeting via PMax.

Ad Group 4 — Competitor Terms

Competitor keywords tend to be bottom-of-funnel by nature — users are already in research mode and comparison shopping. These convert at a higher rate than generic terms. Test a separate ad group targeting competitor brand names.

Question for you: What percentage of your clients are grad school vs. undergraduate? This directly shapes how much investment we put behind the grad school ad group vs. keeping the primary focus on undergrad BOF terms.

05 — Google Ads PMax Opportunities
Opportunity

Performance Max: Areas to Improve

Your PMax campaign is active and has room to expand reach and improve efficiency with a few targeted adjustments.

Brand Traffic Leakage

Your PMax campaign does not exclude brand traffic. This isn't necessarily wrong, but PMax will naturally optimize toward the easiest conversions — and brand terms convert most easily. This means PMax may be cannibalizing budget that should belong to your brand search campaign, while underinvesting in non-branded reach.

Test: Add a brand exclusion list to PMax and monitor whether non-brand impression volume increases without a significant drop in overall conversions.

Age Exclusion Mismatch

The current PMax campaign excludes ages 18–34. This makes sense for an undergrad parent-focused campaign — but if you want to pursue a grad school audience, those users (who may be 22–30 and researching for themselves) are being excluded entirely.

Test: Create a separate PMax campaign or asset group targeting ages 22–35, with grad-school-specific creatives and landing pages.

07 — Google Ads Future Expansion
Future Opportunity

YouTube & Connected TV

Once search and PMax are optimized, YouTube represents a compelling upper-funnel expansion channel — particularly for reaching parents in a high-attention environment.

08 — Meta Creative Strategy
Highest Leverage

Meta: Creative Strategy

Creative is the single biggest lever in Meta performance. Your audience — primarily parents of teenagers — is a natural fit for Facebook's demographic. The opportunity is in developing a systematic testing approach across multiple angles and formats.

😰

"Is your child's application actually competitive?"

Parent anxiety angle. Speaks directly to the fear of leaving college admissions to chance. High engagement on Facebook.

🏆

"Our students are 3× more likely to get into schools under 15% acceptance"

Outcome and results hook. Specific, credible, and immediately differentiates Prepory from self-guided applicants.

❌

"The biggest mistake families make when applying to top schools"

Myth-busting format. Strong thumb-stopper. Triggers curiosity before showing the solution.

⏰

"If your student is in 10th grade, here's what needs to happen now"

Urgency tied to grade level. Hyper-relevant to parents — feel like the ad was written for them specifically.

Format Recommendation

  • 30–60 second video from a counselor or former admissions officer builds credibility fast
  • You have one video that will work well — need to expand the library
  • Pair video with static image ads testing different headline angles
  • UGC-style (parent testimonial) is worth testing — high trust signal

What to Test First

  • Test 2–3 creative angles simultaneously in a broad cold audience
  • Let Meta's algo identify a winner before narrowing further
  • Once you have a winning angle, create 3+ variations of that hook
  • Seasonal hooks tied to decision deadlines should refresh every quarter
09 — Meta Audience & Campaign Structure
Campaign Build

Audience Strategy & Campaign Structure

A structured Meta funnel separates cold audiences from warm ones, ensures you're always prospecting, and retargets engaged users with a conversion-focused ask.

Cold Audience Targeting

  • Parents of 13–17 year olds — Facebook demographic targeting
  • Interest: SAT/ACT prep, college planning, private school, parenting teens
  • Lookalike from past clients/leads — prioritize once list is large enough

Warm Audience Retargeting

  • Site visitors (all pages, last 30/60/90 days)
  • Video viewers (25%, 50%, 75% thresholds)
  • Lead form openers who didn't submit
  • Webinar registrants who didn't book a consultation

Key Principle

Always keep cold and warm audiences in separate campaigns — they need different creative, different objectives, and different budget logic. Mixing them gives you blended data that makes optimization impossible.
